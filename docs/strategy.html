How to use this

Before your pick: open Draft Plan (your slot's per-pick board) and Draft Assistant (best available vs. your roster). This page is the why behind those — the plan for each phase and how to adjust when the board doesn't cooperate. Read "The one rule" and "Draft shape" before Monday; keep "Reacting live" and the lists open during.

The one rule

Draft the best player available within tiers, weighted to your roster needs. Never reach more than about half a round past a player's ADP. If two players are close, break the tie toward (1) the scarcer position — RB before WR — and (2) the better situation / higher ceiling.

Tiers > exact rank. If a position's tier is about to empty and it's a while until your next pick, take the last player in that tier now even if someone at another position grades a hair higher. The drop to the next tier is the real cost.

Draft shape by round (12-team half-PPR, 2026)

The 2026 consensus is "Modified Hero RB": lock one bell-cow RB early, then attack the very deep WR pool, and treat rounds 3–6 at RB as a minefield. Zero-RB (no RB until round 7+) is not favored this year — the mid-round RB values dried up and the elite-RB miss is unrecoverable.

RoundPriorityNotes
1Elite RB or elite WR Picks 1–4: take a top RB (Gibbs, Bijan, J. Taylor). Picks 5–12: best WR (Chase, Nacua, ARSB, JSN) or the falling elite RB. An elite TE (Bowers/McBride) is defensible at the 1/2 turn only.
2–3WR, WR The deep position — this is where you build strength. One of the two picks can be the other elite TE if you missed at the turn. Avoid RB2 here unless a top-10 RB fell.
4–6RB Dead Zone — value only RB hit rates crater: ~35% (Rd 4) → ~24% (Rd 5–6) → ~18% (Rd 7). Don't auto-draft RBs here. Take them only at a clear ADP discount or for a role reason (passing-down work, an injury ahead of them on the depth chart). Otherwise keep taking WR value and your first TE if elite.
6–8QB (if you want a top-6), RB depth, TE if punted Maye / Burrow / Hurts / Daniels land here. Also the last shot at a starting-caliber RB2 before it's committee roulette.

Rough round math: Round ≈ ADP ÷ 12. A player with ADP 40 goes around the 3/4 turn.

Adjusting by draft slot

  • Picks 1–3 (turn at 1/2): You get an elite RB, then wait ~22 picks and pick back-to-back. Take the safest floor at 1.xx, then draft in pairs at the turn — two WRs, or WR + elite TE. Don't count on a specific player surviving the long gap; plan around tiers.
  • Picks 4–6: Best mix of value and cadence. Likely an elite WR at 1.xx (or JT/CMC if they slide), then a steady ~11-pick rhythm. Just take best-available-in-tier every time.
  • Picks 7–9: The elite-RB tier may be gone — be ready to open RB or WR, and to go WR-WR-WR if that's how the board falls. Your R2/R3 is a strong RB2 window (Walker, Henry, Barkley range).
  • Picks 10–12 (turn at 1/2): You pick back-to-back at the 1/2 turn, then wait ~22. Great spot to grab an RB + elite WR pair, or WR + elite TE. Then long wait — bank two you love.

Reacting live to what your opponents do

A run just happened (3+ of one position in the last ~5 picks)

  • You need that position: jump in now — the run is pulling the next tier forward, and waiting means a worse player at a worse cost.
  • You don't: do the opposite — everyone just spent capital elsewhere, so the position they skipped is now discounted. Take the value there.

A tier is about to break

  • Count how many players remain in the current tier at each position you might want, and how many picks until you're back up. If players left ≤ picks until your next turn, that tier will be gone — take the last one now.

It's your pick and nobody jumps out

  • Best value in the highest tier available. Tie-break toward RB (scarcer), then toward the player whose team/role gives a higher ceiling.
  • Don't draft your K, DST, or a backup QB while any startable RB/WR/flex option remains.
  • Check byes: if 3+ of your projected starters already share a bye week, take the equal alternative on a different bye.

Where our board and the crowd disagree

  • The board's projections are single-source (ESPN). When our model rank and ADP diverge hard with no concrete reason (no injury, no lost role), lean to ADP — it's ~2,900 real drafts. When there is a concrete reason (see Fades / Injuries), trust the reason.

Quarterback plan

Single-QB, 4pt passing TD — QB is replaceable. Ignore the board's QB "rank" column entirely (VBD overrates them and the projections are noisy); use ADP and these tiers.

  • Elite (only if it falls to you ~Rd 3): Josh Allen (ADP ~31). Clear PPG leader. Fine value at that price — never earlier.
  • Strong, Rd 5–8: Joe Burrow (~54), Drake Maye (~52), Lamar Jackson (~56), Jalen Hurts (~82), Jayden Daniels (~74). Any one of these is plenty. Target the last of the tier.
  • Wait / stream, Rd 9+: Patrick Mahomes (~104), Brock Purdy (~86), Trevor Lawrence (~92), Jared Goff (~101), Bo Nix (~112), Kyler Murray (~133). Pair two late and play matchups.
  • Dart throws: Jaxson Dart (~123), Tyler Shough (~143) — see Upside.

Best outcome: be one of the first (Allen at value) or one of the last to take a QB. Avoid the middle scramble.

Tight end plan

Two elite, a big gap, then a deep streamable pool. Either pay up early or wait a long time — the middle is the trap.

  • Elite (Rd 3–4): Brock Bowers (~40), Trey McBride (~39). Weekly positional advantage. McBride carries mild regression risk (169 targets unlikely to repeat, ARI added Jeremiyah Love) — still a top-2 TE.
  • Next up (Rd 5–7): Colston Loveland (~58), Tyler Warren (~69) — ascending, cheaper.
  • Wait pool (Rd 8+): George Kittle (~90), Sam LaPorta (~110), Dallas Goedert (~119), Mark Andrews (~134), Jake Ferguson (~147). Our model likes several of these a lot more than their ADP — good "wait on TE" insurance.
  • Punt target (Rd 13+): Juwan Johnson (~155), last year's TE8 if you completely wait.

Kicker & defense

  • Last two rounds only. No exceptions.
  • K: take one of the accurate, good-offense kickers by ADP — Brandon Aubrey, Jason Myers, Ka'imi Fairbairn, Cameron Dicker. Then never think about it — stream on bye.
  • DST: our board has no defense ADP, so pick a Week 1 home favorite vs. a weak offense and stream weekly off matchup. Don't pay up for a "name" defense.
